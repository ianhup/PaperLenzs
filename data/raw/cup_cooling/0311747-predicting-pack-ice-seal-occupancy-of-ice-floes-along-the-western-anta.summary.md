## Title
Predicting pack-ice seal occupancy of ice floes along the Western Antarctic Peninsula

## Topics
Sea ice and seal occupancy

## Introduction
In this study, we use Very High-Resolution (VHR) satellite imagery to both detect individual pack-ice seals and to examine the sea ice features on which seals are hauled out.

## Methods
Mosaicing these scenes allowed us to retain more ice floes in the dataset, ensuring that we minimized data loss due to overlaps or the exclusion of floes near the scene edges.

## Results
Segmented regression analysis reveals significant breakpoints in seal count trends at ice floe areas of 49 m2 and 481 m2.

## Discussion
This suggests that while seals clearly need sea ice to haul out, denser sea ice may discourage them from doing so, possibly because it restricts their physical movement and limits access to open water and haul-out sites.

## Conclusion
The satellite-image model linked haul-out abundance to floe size, nearby ice concentration and marine topography. Estimates were more reliable across an entire image scene than for individual floes, supporting further development of automated seal surveys.
