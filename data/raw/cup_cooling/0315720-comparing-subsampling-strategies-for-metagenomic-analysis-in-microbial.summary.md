## Title
Comparing subsampling strategies for metagenomic analysis in microbial studies using amplicon sequence variants versus operational taxonomic units

## Topics
Metagenomics and subsampling

## Introduction
Through clustering of similar sequences, the OTU approach aims to minimize the effect of possible sequencing errors.

## Methods
We accessed 3,349 samples (16S rRNA and/or SM-seq) from the DIABIMMUNE microbiome project (comprising of three cohort studies investigating the gut microbiota of infants) [20] alongside the subject metadata.

## Results
Extreme and SPCA subsampling both display very different subsamples between ASVs and OTUs at low sample sizes (less than 50% overlap), interestingly the overlap was shown to increase as sample size was increased.

## Discussion
This may in part be due to the difference in the 16S rRNA processing.

## Conclusion
Using amplicon sequence variants generally produced subsamples with similar bacterial representation to those selected using operational taxonomic units. Selection agreement ranged from 50% to 93% across the methods evaluated, so the two approaches did not always select the same samples.
