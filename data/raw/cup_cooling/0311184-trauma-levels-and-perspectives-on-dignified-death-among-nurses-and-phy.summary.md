## Title
Trauma levels and perspectives on dignified death among nurses and physicians who directly experienced the recent earthquake

## Topics
Earthquake trauma and healthcare professionals

## Introduction
The aim of this study is to determine the levels of trauma experienced by physicians and nurses who directly experienced the earthquake and their perceptions of a good death, and to understand the relationship between these factors.

## Methods
The questionnaire forms included appropriate scales to assess the participants’ levels of trauma and their views on the concept of a good death.

## Results
A significant majority, 90.4% of the physicians and 92.1% of the nurses, were still working in the earthquake-affected areas.

## Discussion
According to the obtained data, there were significant differences between physicians and nurses in terms of the subdimensions of the trauma scale, namely "emotional limitation," "emotional," "cognitive restructuring," and "sleep problems" scores (p<0.05).

## Conclusion
Such research will illuminate the comprehensive effects of trauma on the overall well-being of healthcare workers and contribute to the development of effective support strategies.
