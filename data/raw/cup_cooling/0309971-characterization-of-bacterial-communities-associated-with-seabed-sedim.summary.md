## Title
Characterization of bacterial communities associated with seabed sediments in offshore and nearshore sites to improve Microbiologically Influenced Corrosion mitigation on marine infrastructures

## Topics
Marine corrosion and sediment microbes

## Introduction
To prevent and limit MIC in marine infrastructures, multiple strategies are currently in use aiming at fighting the adhesion and biofilm formation of the microorganisms on the surfaces.

## Methods
For each sediment sample, three DNA extractions were performed and pooled.

## Results
The rarefaction curves showed that the sequencing provided sufficient depth to investigate the microbial community composition and diversity (S1 Fig).

## Discussion
Among the most abundant phyla, Bacteroidota and Firmicutes were associated with T sediments and their members suggested high human activity compared to N sediments.

## Conclusion
The enrichment of human-associated and sulfate-reducing bacterial members in Trieste demonstrated that this site is featured with high anthropogenic impact and is more likely to host corrosion events on installed marine infrastructures than the Norwegian site.
