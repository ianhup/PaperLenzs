## Title
Student-centered factors influencing inclusion in biomedical majors among first-year undergraduate students

## Topics
Inclusion in biomedical undergraduate programs

## Introduction
Maintaining a diverse scientific workforce is vital to promoting the nation’s economic competitiveness and position in technological leadership [1–3].

## Methods
No parents or guardians were contacted in the consent process since all participants were 18 or older.

## Results
When the results are statistically significant at 5% or 1% this is denoted via a single or double asterisk (* or **), respectively.

## Discussion
Research suggests that undergraduates who are pushed out are often highly qualified college entrants who are disproportionately women and those from minoritized race and ethnicity groups [75].

## Conclusion
The study found that first-year retention in biomedical majors was associated with both institutional conditions and student characteristics. The authors recommend that institutions address the learning environment alongside student-level support.
