## Title
Challenges and strategies in the soluble expression of CTA1-(S14P5)4-DD and CTA1-(S21P2)4-DD fusion proteins as candidates for COVID-19 intranasal vaccines

## Topics
Vaccine candidates and soluble protein expression

## Introduction
In this study, we aim to construct fusion proteins consisting of tandem repeat epitopes S14P5 or S21P2 with CTA1 and DD, resulting in CTA1-(S14P5)4-DD and CTA1-(S21P2)4-DD fusion proteins, which are prokaryotically expressible, functional, and soluble.

## Methods
The resulting pellet was solubilized in 50 μL of SDS-PAGE sample buffer, and 10 μL of this suspension was loaded into a well of a 15-well polyacrylamide gel of the Mini-PROTEAN II electrophoresis apparatus (Bio-Rad).

## Results
No protein expression was observed before IPTG induction, indicating correct regulation of genetic constructs and a responsive expression system.

## Discussion
The structure of the CTA1-(S14P5)4-DD and CTA1-(S14P5)4-DD components, as predicted by AlphaFold-2, were compatible with their original structure, suggesting the proper function of the proteins.

## Conclusion
Solubility was lower than predicted for both fusion proteins. Changing the timing of induction improved soluble expression for one construct, and mild detergents improved solubility during extraction, but purification yields remained low at 1–2 mg per litre of culture.
