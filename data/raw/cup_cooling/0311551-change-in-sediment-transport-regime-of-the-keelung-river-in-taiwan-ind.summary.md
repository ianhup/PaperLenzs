## Title
Change in sediment transport regime of the Keelung River in Taiwan induced by the operation of Yuanshantze flood diversion tunnel

## Topics
River sediment and flood diversion

## Introduction
The design of the YFDT only considers its impact on flow, and its effect on sediment transport may also be worth investigating.

## Methods
At Wudu discharge gauge, water discharge (Q) was measured daily (or hourly for the typhoon / rainstorm events) and the suspended sediment concentration (Cs) was measured biweekly by using USDH-48 depth-integrated suspended sediment sampler recommended by the Federal Interagency Sedimentation Project of the USA [4].

## Results
In terms of the minimum daily discharge, except for December, which decreased by 1.47 [m3sec-1] (from 2.47 to 1 [m3sec-1]), the minimum daily discharge for the other months increased, with the increment ranging from 0.08 to 1.17 [m3sec-1].

## Discussion
This suggests that the impact of flood diversion tunnel on downstream sediment load may be greater than that of reservoirs.

## Conclusion
In conclusion, while the diversion tunnel addresses immediate human concerns, whether it triggers a series of cascading effects on topography and ecology remains ongoing attention, including the unprecedented influx of a substantial volume of sediment into the East China Sea.
