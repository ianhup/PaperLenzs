## Title
The petting diaries: Investigating owner-cat petting styles, responses and preferences

## Topics
Feline behavior; petting; owner reports; aggression; environmental enrichment; observational study

## Introduction
Petting is a common human-cat interaction, but individual responses and predictors of unwanted scratching or biting are poorly understood.

## Methods
Sixty-six owners completed a one-day log covering three petting sessions, together with survey information about cats, owners, households, and lifetime responses. Logistic and mixed logistic regressions examined associations with recorded and historical reactions.

## Results
Most cats reportedly enjoyed logged sessions, and no significant predictors emerged for those session responses. Lifetime owner scratching or biting was associated with verbal correction (OR 14.2) and stopping petting after previous incidents (OR 6.9), while exploratory enrichment was associated with lower odds (OR 0.15).

## Discussion
Wide confidence intervals, owner reporting, small samples, and uncertain directionality limit causal interpretation. Stopping petting may be a response to an already reactive cat; the association does not show that continuing unwanted petting is beneficial.

## Conclusion
Better recognition of feline preferences and warning signals, alongside further prospective research, may improve interactions and welfare.

## Relevance to research
Not directly relevant to the mockup. Domestic-cat handling differs from bee habitat use and community diversity. Mixed models and observational-causality cautions are transferable methods lessons, not evidence about green-roof effects.
