## Title
The appeals of communism: Extracting confessions from the communist subject in the Vietnam war

## Topics
Historical geography; interrogation; confession; RAND; Vietnam War; colonial power; archival research

## Introduction
The article investigates how assumptions about communism and psychological confession shaped American military intelligence practices during the Vietnam War.

## Methods
Archival research on RAND's 1964-1968 Motivation and Morale Project is interpreted through theoretical perspectives associated with Arendt, Fanon, and Foucault. The analysis connects prisoner interviews and ex-communist confessional literature to broader histories of power and interrogation.

## Results
The author argues that efforts to extract a recognizable communist personality and psychological truth generated distorted representations of Vietnamese prisoners and defectors, reinforcing military assumptions rather than providing neutral knowledge.

## Discussion
The findings are historical and interpretive, not statistical estimates of interrogation effectiveness. Connections to later detention and torture practices illuminate continuities but do not make every setting identical.

## Conclusion
Confessional intelligence practices should be understood within the ideological and colonial relationships shaping both the questions asked and the evidence produced.
