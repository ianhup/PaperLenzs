## Title
Institutional Retention of US MD-Granting Medical School Graduates for Graduate Medical Education: A National, Cross-Specialty Study

## Topics
Medical education; residency; institutional retention; administrative records; multilevel logistic regression

## Introduction
Graduates may enter residency programs affiliated with their own medical schools, but national patterns and associated characteristics vary across specialties and institutions.

## Methods
A retrospective cross-sectional analysis linked national student, residency application, and graduate medical education databases for five graduating cohorts from 2018-2019 through 2022-2023. Among eligible graduates, multilevel logistic models estimated adjusted associations with institutional retention.

## Results
The analytic sample included 69,860 graduates, of whom 16,927 (24.2%) were retained. Retention ranged from 18.8% in emergency medicine to 48.1% in integrated thoracic surgery, and was associated with cohort, application counts, and institutional characteristics.

## Discussion
The associations reflect a combination of student preferences, program opportunities, and institutional context; they do not identify causal effects of application strategy. Eligibility depended on the availability of an affiliated program in the graduate's specialty.

## Conclusion
Institutional retention has become more common and differs substantially across specialties, providing benchmarks for medical education planning.
