## Title
Cats and humans show correlated affiliative physiological states

## Topics
Human-cat relationships; oxytocin; cortisol; proximity; mood; observational physiology

## Introduction
Hormonal associations in human-dog relationships are better studied than those involving cats. This exploratory study examines owner-cat physiological associations and their links to mood and proximity.

## Methods
Nineteen owners and 29 cats were monitored over one month. Urinary oxytocin and cortisol measurements were paired with owner mood reports, Bluetooth-based proximity estimates, and exploratory feline social-approach tests.

## Results
Owner and cat oxytocin concentrations were positively associated, whereas cortisol concentrations showed little association. Higher cat oxytocin accompanied lower owner cortisol and less negative mood; closer detected proximity predicted owner vigor. Cat hormones related differently to social approach.

## Discussion
These are correlations, potentially reflecting shared household conditions rather than dyad-specific effects. Small sample size, several cats sharing owners, incomplete independence, and limitations of Bluetooth distance estimates restrict interpretation.

## Conclusion
The findings suggest oxytocin-related associations in human-cat affiliation, requiring larger confirmatory studies to distinguish mechanisms and shared-environment effects.
