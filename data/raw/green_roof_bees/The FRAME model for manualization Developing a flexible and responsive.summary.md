## Title
The FRAME model for manualization: Developing a flexible and responsive telehealth art therapy approach for military PTSD

## Topics
Art therapy; PTSD; veterans; telehealth; treatment manualization; clinical decision support

## Introduction
Creative arts therapists may resist treatment manuals if standardization restricts individualized care. The article describes a framework intended to combine treatment fidelity with clinical flexibility.

## Methods
An interdisciplinary team, including art therapists, psychologists, physicians, and veterans, developed the eight-week ART-VET telehealth program. The paper describes the FRAME development process, treatment resources, and a clinical decision-making guide for tailoring session topics.

## Results
The resulting protocol combines shared session components with individualized choices, including trauma tolerance, processing, integration, and flexible sessions. Its outputs are the intervention structure and manualization framework; controlled treatment-effectiveness results are not reported.

## Discussion
The proposed balance between standardization and adaptation could support reproducibility while preserving responsiveness. Its utility, fidelity, and clinical benefits require the planned pilot and subsequent empirical testing.

## Conclusion
FRAME offers a structured approach to designing flexible manualized art therapy, illustrated through ART-VET rather than established by a completed efficacy trial.
