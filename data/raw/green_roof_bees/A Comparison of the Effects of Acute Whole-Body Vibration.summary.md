## Title
A Comparison of the Effects of Acute Whole-Body Vibration for Relieving Vocal Fatigue in Young and Older Adults

## Topics
Vocal fatigue; whole-body vibration; aging; voice recovery; intervention duration

## Introduction
The study asks whether whole-body vibration is associated with relief of experimentally induced vocal fatigue in younger and older women, and whether recovery varies with age and exposure duration.

## Methods
Eighteen younger and 18 older women with healthy voices completed karaoke singing to induce fatigue, followed by 15 minutes of whole-body vibration. Highest fundamental frequency, perceived vocal effort, and fatigue were assessed before and after loading, during vibration, and 15 minutes after treatment.

## Results
Both age groups reported significant fatigue reduction after five minutes of vibration. Duration and age-by-duration effects were significant for perceived effort and fatigue; older participants' recovery slowed after ten minutes. Highest fundamental frequency differed by age.

## Discussion
A rest-only control group was absent, so natural recovery and elapsed time cannot be separated from vibration effects. Self-reported outcomes, the small female-only sample, and limited objective measures constrain causal interpretation and generalization.

## Conclusion
Whole-body vibration is a promising approach to fatigue relief across age groups, but controlled comparisons are needed to establish its specific contribution.
