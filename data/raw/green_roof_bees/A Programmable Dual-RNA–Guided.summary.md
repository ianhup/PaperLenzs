## Title
A Programmable Dual-RNA-Guided DNA Endonuclease in Adaptive Bacterial Immunity

## Topics
CRISPR-Cas9; bacterial immunity; RNA-guided cleavage; nuclease domains; genome editing

## Introduction
The article investigates how type-II CRISPR systems recognize and cleave foreign DNA and whether their targeting machinery can be simplified into a programmable tool.

## Methods
Purified Streptococcus pyogenes Cas9 was tested with crRNA, tracrRNA, plasmid and short DNA substrates. Cleavage mapping, nuclease-domain mutants, RNA modifications, target mismatches, and engineered single-guide RNA chimeras established functional requirements.

## Results
Cas9 required both crRNA and tracrRNA for sequence-specific double-stranded DNA cleavage. HNH and RuvC-like domains cleaved opposite strands, and recognition depended on guide complementarity and an adjacent PAM. A fused RNA chimera also directed cleavage of chosen DNA targets.

## Discussion
The experiments establish biochemical programmability and explain recognition requirements. Genome editing is proposed as an application; this paper does not itself demonstrate efficient editing of organisms in natural populations.

## Conclusion
Dual-RNA-guided Cas9 is a programmable DNA endonuclease that can be redirected using a single engineered guide RNA.
