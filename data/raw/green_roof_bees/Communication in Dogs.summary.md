## Title
Communication in Dogs

## Topics
Canine communication; visual signals; vocalization; olfaction; touch; brain lateralization

## Introduction
Dogs communicate with other dogs and humans through a flexible repertoire shaped by domestication and social experience. This review examines the forms and meanings of those signals.

## Methods
The authors synthesize published behavioral and neuroscience studies of visual, acoustic, olfactory, and tactile communication, including evidence about lateralized processing. This is a narrative review, not a new controlled experiment.

## Results
The reviewed literature describes context-dependent information in posture, gaze, facial movements, tail movements, vocalizations, odors, and physical contact. Some signals have different meanings in dog-dog and dog-human interactions, while breeding-related morphology can constrain signaling.

## Discussion
Individual signals require interpretation in their behavioral context. The communicative importance of particular body regions remains incompletely studied, motivating further work on gaze and olfactory attention.

## Conclusion
Understanding multiple signaling channels and their context can improve interpretation of canine social behavior and human-dog interactions.
