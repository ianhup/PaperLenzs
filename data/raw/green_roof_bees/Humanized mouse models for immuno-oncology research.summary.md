## Title
Humanized mouse models for immuno-oncology research

## Topics
Humanized mice; immunotherapy; tumor microenvironment; immune engraftment; preclinical models

## Introduction
Preclinical systems often fail to reproduce interactions between human tumors and immune cells, complicating evaluation of human-specific cancer immunotherapies.

## Methods
This review compares immunodeficient mice engrafted with human tumors and immune components. It examines immune-cell sources, host genetic modifications, innate-cell support, and strategies for matching patient-derived tumors with immune cells.

## Results
Published studies show that humanized models can support investigation of tumor-immune interactions and immunotherapy responses. Advances improve particular human immune populations, but no single model reproduces the full human immune environment.

## Discussion
Incomplete engraftment, mismatched components, variable responses, and species-specific biology limit clinical translation. The article synthesizes existing evidence rather than presenting a new head-to-head efficacy trial.

## Conclusion
Model selection should reflect the immunological question, and further improvements in engraftment and patient matching are needed to improve translational value.
