## Title
Building parent-child connectedness through arts, storytelling, and multi-sensory experiences: An asset for preventing family violence

## Topics
Creative arts therapies; immigrant families; connectedness; emotional regulation; community intervention

## Introduction
Pandemic-related stress compounded existing pressures on immigrant families in Montreal. The article explores how a community arts program may strengthen protective family relationships.

## Methods
A multiple-case qualitative study examined two families within the Art et Contes en famille program. Eight to ten workshops integrated storytelling, sensory play, artmaking, and sharing. Therapist observation notes and family artworks were interpreted through connection, autonomy, and regulation.

## Results
The cases illustrate moments of improved joint engagement, mutual influence, opportunities for children's autonomy, and emotional expression and regulation. Changes varied across interactions and family members rather than following one uniform trajectory.

## Discussion
Therapist observations and selected case material provide rich process descriptions but do not establish causal reductions in family violence. The small case selection, interpretive analysis, and absence of a controlled outcome comparison limit generalization.

## Conclusion
Accessible expressive-arts activities may support parent-child connectedness and warrant further evaluation as part of family-violence prevention efforts.
