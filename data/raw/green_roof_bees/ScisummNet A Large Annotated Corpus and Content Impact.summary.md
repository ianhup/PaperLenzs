## Title
ScisummNet: A Large Annotated Corpus and Content-Impact Models for Scientific Paper Summarization with Citation Networks

## Topics
Scientific summarization; annotated corpus; citation networks; neural summarization; content and impact

## Introduction
Scientific papers are lengthy and technical, while summaries ideally capture both author-emphasized contributions and contributions recognized by later researchers. Existing annotated datasets were small.

## Methods
The authors constructed expert summaries for 1,000 computational-linguistics papers using abstracts and incoming citation sentences, with validation of the annotation procedure. Neural hybrid models combined abstracts with citation-linked source passages and citation information, evaluated using shared-task data and human judgments.

## Results
The larger corpus improved supervised summarization performance, and hybrid approaches produced summaries covering aspects missed by abstracts or citation-only summaries. The study demonstrated the value of combining author and research-community perspectives.

## Discussion
Citation-based signals can emphasize influential contributions but depend on citation availability, domain, and extraction quality. The dataset is concentrated in computational linguistics and does not directly validate generalization to ecological papers.

## Conclusion
ScisummNet provides a substantial training resource and a content-plus-impact framework for scientific-paper summarization.

## Relevance to research
Not ecological evidence for the mockup, but directly useful to the broader paper-reading-model project. It informs summary annotation and evaluation. Its citation-network methods do not establish whether green roofs increase bee abundance or species richness.
