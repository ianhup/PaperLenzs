## Title
Multifrequency-STD NMR unveils the first Michaelis complex of an intramolecular trans-sialidase from Ruminococcus gnavus

## Topics
Enzyme-substrate recognition; STD NMR; sialoglycans; gut microbiota; molecular docking; structural models

## Introduction
The gut bacterium Ruminococcus gnavus uses a specialized enzyme to obtain metabolites from intestinal mucin. The study investigates the structural basis of its substrate recognition and linkage specificity.

## Methods
Saturation-transfer-difference NMR, competition and variable-temperature experiments, DEEP-STD mapping, molecular docking, and CORCEMA-ST calculations examined sialoglycan binding to the RgNanH catalytic domain and validated structural models in solution.

## Results
Both 3'- and 6'-sialyllactose bound, but with different affinity and kinetics. The proposed Michaelis-complex model places the 3'-linked substrate's galactose against Trp698 through a CH-pi interaction; the geometry of the 6'-linked compound prevents the corresponding contact.

## Discussion
Binding alone does not imply catalytic turnover. The model explains why a recognized ligand may not be a substrate, while remaining a solution-validated computational model rather than an enzyme-substrate crystal structure.

## Conclusion
Combined NMR and computational methods clarify the enzyme's preference for alpha-2,3-linked substrates and its molecular recognition mechanism.
