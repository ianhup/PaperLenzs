## Title
Root-associated microbial community dynamics in tobacco following topping: implications for nicotine degradation

## Topics
Rhizosphere; microbial communities; tobacco; nicotine degradation; amplicon sequencing; metabolomics

## Introduction
Nicotine accumulation following agricultural topping may create environmental burdens. The study examines temporal microbial and metabolic patterns associated with tobacco roots and identifies potential nicotine-degrading bacteria.

## Methods
Amplicon sequencing compared bulk soil, rhizosphere, and root-associated communities over post-topping stages. Metabolomic and phytohormone measurements were integrated with microbial profiles, followed by high-throughput isolation and functional testing of representative bacteria.

## Results
Communities shifted over time, with nicotine-related metabolites and candidate degrading genera showing associations. Cultivation recovered 221 rhizosphere and 197 root nicotine-utilizing isolates. Five representative strains were validated, with particularly strong performance in Ensifer A4 and Pseudomonas B11.

## Discussion
The field sampling lacked matched untopped controls, so temporal associations cannot demonstrate causal topping effects. Laboratory degradation assays support strain function but do not alone demonstrate remediation effectiveness in field ecosystems.

## Conclusion
Root-associated microbial dynamics identify promising organisms for further nicotine-bioremediation research while leaving causal field relationships unresolved.

## Relevance to research
Indirect ecological and methodological relevance. Habitat compartments, environmental filtering, and community composition are useful concepts for interpreting roof communities. However, tobacco microbes and nicotine degradation differ from wild bees and floral resources, and cannot validate green-roof abundance or richness effects.
