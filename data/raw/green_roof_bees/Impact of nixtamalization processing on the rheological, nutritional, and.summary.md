## Title
Impact of nixtamalization processing on the rheological, nutritional, and physicochemical properties of tortillas made from nixtamalized maize-bean flours

## Topics
Nixtamalization; bean fortification; tortilla composition; starch hydrolysis; texture; calcium salts

## Introduction
Adding beans to maize tortillas may improve nutritional composition, while processing chemistry can change mineral content, starch behavior, and food texture.

## Methods
The study compared traditional and ecological nixtamalization and water cooking across four bean types. Tortillas used 50:50 bean-maize flour blends and underwent compositional, mineral, color, viscosity, texture, and in-vitro starch-digestion analyses against controls.

## Results
Fortified formulations contained approximately 14.61-18.11 g protein per 100 g. Processing primarily drove calcium enrichment, whereas bean type affected macronutrients. Calcium chloride favored faster hydrolysis and resistant-starch levels up to 5.04 g/100 g; formulations also differed in softness and color.

## Discussion
In-vitro digestibility is not a measured human glycemic response. Sensory acceptance was not tested, and nutritional advantages must be considered alongside processing-related nutrient losses and technological properties.

## Conclusion
Bean selection and calcium-salt processing provide complementary ways to modify tortilla nutrition and functionality, warranting consumer and further biological evaluation.

Not relevant to the mockup's ecological question. Food-processing effects on cooked maize-bean products do not address flowering vegetation, bee foraging, nesting substrate, or green-roof biodiversity.
