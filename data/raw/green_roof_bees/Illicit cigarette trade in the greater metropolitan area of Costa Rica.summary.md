## Title
Illicit cigarette trade in the greater metropolitan area of Costa Rica: a smokers' survey

## Topics
Tobacco; illicit trade; Costa Rica; consumption estimates; market concentration; pack inspection

## Introduction
Independent estimates of illicit cigarette markets are limited in Central America, where supply chains and enforcement may be as important as consumer affordability.

## Methods
A geographically stratified intercept survey with sex-age quotas included 613 adult smokers in November-December 2023. Photographic pack inspection and a prespecified price rule for single cigarettes classified the most recent purchase; sensitivity analyses varied price thresholds.

## Results
Illicit cigarettes represented an estimated 30.5% of consumption (95% CI 25.3-35.7), while 20.3% of smokers reported an illicit last purchase. Pack purchases drove the estimate, and the illicit market was concentrated in a few unregistered brands.

## Discussion
The non-probability design and restriction to the metropolitan area limit national generalization. The observed concentration is consistent with organized supply pathways, but the survey does not directly reconstruct trafficking networks.

## Conclusion
The findings support attention to customs oversight and supply-chain controls, alongside transparent independent measurement.
