## Title
Number of Children and Nine-Year Health Trajectories Among Older Adults in China: The Role of Social Participation

## Topics
Family size; aging; self-rated health; functional limitations; social participation; longitudinal modeling

## Introduction
Larger families may provide support but also impose cumulative burdens. The study examines associations between number of children and later-life health, including whether social participation moderates those associations.

## Methods
Five waves of the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study from 2011 to 2020 included 5,788 respondents aged at least 60 with a living child. Mixed-effects ordinal logistic models assessed self-rated health, instrumental daily-living limitations, and interactions with time and baseline social participation.

## Results
More children were associated with poorer self-rated health (OR 1.080), greater functional limitations (OR 1.070), and faster accumulation of limitations (OR 1.013 per year). Social participation accompanied better health, but its moderation of the family-size/self-rated-health association was sensitive to specification.

## Discussion
Social participation did not significantly moderate functional-health associations or rates of change. Observational associations and baseline participation measurement do not establish that increasing participation reverses family-related disadvantages.

## Conclusion
Older parents with more children may face health disadvantages; intervention studies are needed to test protective strategies.
