## Title
Observation of Gravitational Waves from a Binary Black Hole Merger

## Topics
Gravitational waves; binary black holes; LIGO; matched filtering; general relativity

## Introduction
General relativity predicts gravitational radiation from accelerating massive systems. Direct detection would enable observation of mergers inaccessible through ordinary electromagnetic measurements.

## Methods
The two LIGO interferometers recorded a coincident transient on September 14, 2015. Matched-filter searches and independent waveform analysis compared the data with relativistic merger predictions, assessed backgrounds, and inferred source parameters while evaluating instrumental and environmental explanations.

## Results
GW150914 had matched-filter signal-to-noise ratio 24 and significance above 5.1 sigma. Source-frame component masses were approximately 36 and 29 solar masses, producing a remnant of about 62 solar masses and radiating roughly three solar masses of energy in gravitational waves.

## Discussion
The waveform matched inspiral, merger, and ringdown predictions and provided a strong-field test of relativity. Distance and source parameters retained uncertainties, and formation pathways were not uniquely identified.

## Conclusion
The event provided the first direct gravitational-wave detection and observation of a binary black-hole merger.
