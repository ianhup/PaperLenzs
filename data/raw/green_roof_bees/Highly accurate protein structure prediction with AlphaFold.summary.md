## Title
Highly accurate protein structure prediction with AlphaFold

## Topics
Protein structure prediction; deep learning; multiple-sequence alignments; geometric learning; CASP14

## Introduction
Experimental structure determination covers only a small fraction of known proteins. The study develops a model to predict three-dimensional structures accurately from amino-acid sequences and evolutionary information.

## Methods
AlphaFold combines learned sequence and residue-pair representations with an end-to-end structure module and physical/geometric inductive biases. Training uses protein-structure data, with blind evaluation at CASP14 and additional testing on structures deposited after the training cutoff.

## Results
Median backbone error on CASP domains was 0.96 angstrom, compared with 2.8 angstrom for the next-best method under the reported metric. Predicted confidence measures tracked structural accuracy, and strong performance extended beyond the competition set.

## Discussion
The model predicts structures resembling experimentally observed conformations, rather than fully describing folding dynamics or all environmental conditions. Confidence and biological context remain important when interpreting predictions.

## Conclusion
Integrating evolutionary information and geometric constraints enables highly accurate protein-structure prediction at a scale impractical for experimental determination alone.
