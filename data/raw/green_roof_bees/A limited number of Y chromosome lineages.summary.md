## Title
A limited number of Y chromosome lineages is present in North American Holsteins

## Topics
Holstein cattle; Y chromosomes; paternal pedigrees; artificial selection; genetic diversity

## Introduction
Intensive dairy breeding may reduce effective population size and genetic diversity. This study examines the extent of paternal-lineage contraction and variation in Y-linked gene families in Holstein bulls.

## Methods
The authors analyzed paternal pedigrees for 62,897 North American bulls born from 1950 to 2013 and 220,872 bulls in international genetic evaluations. Copy-number variation in PRAMEY, HSFY, and ZNF280BY was evaluated in 257 bulls to compare ancestral lineages.

## Results
Current North American artificial-insemination bulls traced to two ancestral bulls born in 1880. Three dominant pedigrees contributed 48.78%, 51.06%, and 0.16% in the 2010s. Gene-family copy numbers varied within lineages but did not differ significantly between the two ancestral lineages.

## Discussion
Artificial selection and widespread artificial insemination are implicated in paternal-lineage loss. Pedigree contraction and gene-copy variation describe different aspects of diversity; direct consequences for reproductive performance require further investigation.

## Conclusion
Holstein Y-chromosome ancestry is extremely concentrated, motivating further study of Y-linked variation and its possible breeding implications.

## Relevance to research
Not directly relevant. Genetic diversity within a domesticated cattle breed is distinct from wild bee species richness across rooftops. The paper offers a broad conservation analogy about diversity loss but does not examine urban habitats, floral cover, roof height, or pollinators.
