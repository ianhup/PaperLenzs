## Title
Prospect Theory: An Analysis of Decision under Risk

## Topics
Decision theory; risk; reference points; loss aversion; certainty effect; probability weighting

## Introduction
Expected utility theory was widely used to describe decisions under risk, but systematic preference patterns suggested that it was descriptively incomplete.

## Methods
The authors present a series of hypothetical choice problems, summarize response patterns, and develop a formal alternative based on editing prospects and evaluating gains and losses relative to a reference point with nonlinear decision weights.

## Results
Choices exhibited certainty, reflection, and isolation effects inconsistent with standard expected-utility assumptions. The proposed value function is typically concave for gains, convex for losses, and steeper for losses; decision weights differ from objective probabilities.

## Discussion
The framework explains why framing and reference points can change preferences. Hypothetical choices and context-dependent behavior limit direct prediction of every real-world decision; the model is descriptive rather than a universal normative prescription.

## Conclusion
Reference-dependent values and probability weighting provide an alternative account of common decisions under risk.

## Relevance to research
Not directly relevant to the mockup's bee ecology. It might inform a separate study of building owners' adoption decisions, but this paper measures neither green-roof choices nor pollinator outcomes. Behavioral analogies cannot establish habitat effects.
