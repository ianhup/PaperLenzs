## Title
Developers' perceptions of automated code review using a generative AI agent: The case of Gandalf

## Topics
Generative AI; code review; developer perceptions; trust; software architecture; industrial case study

## Introduction
LLM-based code-review tools are increasingly deployed, yet developer perceptions of their usefulness and architectural limitations in industrial settings remain understudied.

## Methods
An exploratory mixed-methods case study examined Gandalf at a Brazilian telecommunications company. Seventeen respondents completed an extended Technology Acceptance Model questionnaire and open-ended questions, complemented by four months of operational data and thematic coding.

## Results
Self-reported behavioral intention averaged 4.37 and perceived usefulness 3.92. Eight respondents described calibrated trust with human validation; ten identified missing context beyond the code diff as a limitation. Perceived architectural effects were indirect.

## Discussion
Acceptance and perceived usefulness do not establish objective improvements in code quality or architecture. The small, single-company sample and diff-only implementation limit transferability, while pointing toward context, memory, and tiered analysis as design needs.

## Conclusion
Developers accepted the agent as a review aid while retaining human oversight and recognizing its limited architectural context.

## Relevance to research
Not relevant as evidence about rooftop bees. It can indirectly inform human validation and context handling in a research-paper-reading application, but its software-review findings do not support green-roof biodiversity claims.
