## Title
Safety and Efficacy of the BNT162b2 mRNA Covid-19 Vaccine

## Topics
Covid-19; mRNA vaccination; randomized trial; vaccine efficacy; short-term safety

## Introduction
The trial evaluated whether a two-dose mRNA vaccine could prevent symptomatic laboratory-confirmed Covid-19 during the initial pandemic period.

## Methods
Participants aged at least 16 were randomized in an observer-blinded multinational trial to two 30-microgram doses of BNT162b2 or placebo, 21 days apart. Efficacy and safety endpoints were assessed under the trial protocol.

## Results
Of 43,548 randomized participants, 43,448 received injections. At least seven days after dose two, eight Covid-19 cases occurred in vaccine recipients and 162 in placebo recipients, yielding 95% efficacy with a 95% credible interval of 90.3-97.6. Common reactions were transient injection-site pain, fatigue, and headache.

## Discussion
The initial median safety follow-up was about two months, limiting detection of rare or long-term events. The estimate concerns the studied population, endpoint, regimen, and pandemic period rather than all future variants or infection outcomes.

## Conclusion
The initial trial demonstrated strong protection against symptomatic Covid-19 with an acceptable short-term safety profile in the studied population.
