## Title
Women and Resistance in the Early Rastafari Movement (book review)

## Topics
Rastafari history; women's agency; gender; Jamaica; historical scholarship; book review

## Introduction
This PDF is Jahlani Niaah's review of Daive A. Dunkley's book, not the book itself or a standalone original research report. It assesses the recovery of women's roles in early Rastafari history.

## Methods
The reviewer discusses the book's use of documents, archival research, interviews, and culturally informed historical interpretation. Those source-gathering methods belong to the reviewed book; the PDF presents critical appraisal rather than a new independently described research sample.

## Results
The review highlights women's leadership, community administration, legal support, religious participation, and cultural work, including figures such as Tenet Bent and Edna Fisher. It identifies the book as a contribution to making women's foundational involvement visible.

## Discussion
The reviewer questions sample representativeness, selection, broad gender interpretations, and whether early Rastafari is too readily treated as an already established religion. These are interpretive criticisms, not statistical results.

## Conclusion
The book is judged an important contribution to women's visibility in Rastafari scholarship while leaving methodological and historical questions open.
