## Title
Sequencing and characterizing human mitochondrial genomes in the biobank-based genomic research paradigm

## Topics
Mitochondrial DNA; heteroplasmy; nuclear mitochondrial segments; biobanks; long-read sequencing; variant analysis

## Introduction
Mitochondrial genomes inform human disease, inheritance, and population history, but accurate analysis is complicated by mixed mitochondrial variants and mitochondrial sequences inserted in the nuclear genome.

## Methods
This review surveys sequencing technologies, computational tools, genomic databases, and biobank-scale studies. It proposes an integrated pipeline for distinguishing authentic mitochondrial variation from nuclear mitochondrial segments using haplotype-resolved and long-read approaches.

## Results
The reviewed advances improve characterization of heteroplasmy, structural variation, and nuclear insertions. They also highlight how technical artifacts can complicate interpretations of apparent paternal inheritance. The proposed pipeline is not presented as a new population-level validation study.

## Discussion
Reliable mapping, representative databases, and integration of multiple data types are central to reducing diagnostic and evolutionary misinterpretation. Emerging sequencing approaches offer opportunities but require careful validation.

## Conclusion
High-quality mitochondrial and nuclear-insertion analysis should combine appropriate sequencing, computational discrimination, and accessible reference resources.
