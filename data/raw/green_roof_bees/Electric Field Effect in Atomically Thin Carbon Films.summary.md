## Title
Electric Field Effect in Atomically Thin Carbon Films

## Topics
Graphene; mechanical exfoliation; two-dimensional transport; field-effect devices; charge carriers

## Introduction
Applying an electric field to control conduction in atomically thin metallic materials had been difficult because thin films often lose continuity and stability.

## Methods
The authors mechanically exfoliated graphite into very thin films, fabricated multi-terminal Hall-bar devices on oxidized silicon, and measured transport under different gate voltages and magnetic-field conditions. More than 60 devices thinner than ten nanometers were studied.

## Results
The films remained stable under ambient conditions and exhibited ambipolar field effects, allowing electron or hole populations up to approximately 10^13 per square centimeter. Reported room-temperature mobilities were around 10,000 square centimeters per volt-second, with submicron ballistic transport.

## Discussion
The findings establish unusually high-quality two-dimensional electronic behavior in thin graphitic films. Device properties and electronic applications are the focus, rather than an environmental field experiment.

## Conclusion
Atomically thin graphene supports gate-controlled electronic transport and offers a platform for studying two-dimensional conductors and novel devices.
