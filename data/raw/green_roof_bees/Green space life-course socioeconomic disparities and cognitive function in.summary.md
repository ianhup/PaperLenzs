## Title
Green space, life-course socioeconomic disparities and cognitive function in later life: A longitudinal analysis

## Topics
Green space; NDVI; cognitive aging; socioeconomic inequality; longitudinal epidemiology

## Introduction
Green space may reduce health inequalities, but its relationship with life-course socioeconomic differences in later-life cognition is uncertain.

## Methods
Two waves of the Northern Ireland Cohort for the Longitudinal Study of Ageing were analyzed, with usable samples of 3,242 and 4,971 participants. Multilevel linear regressions related MMSE scores to NDVI, distance to parks and woodland, socioeconomic indicators, and other covariates.

## Results
MMSE scores were positively associated with NDVI (coefficient 0.51, SE 0.13) and negatively associated with park distance (coefficient -0.09, SE 0.02). Favorable green-space associations were stronger among participants with lower socioeconomic status.

## Discussion
The observational results suggest an equity-related association, not proof that increasing vegetation causes improved cognition. Exposure measurement and differences between socioeconomic groups complicate interpretation.

## Conclusion
Accessible green space may be associated with smaller socioeconomic inequalities in later-life cognitive function.

## Relevance to research
Indirect contextual relevance. Both papers concern benefits associated with vegetation in human-modified landscapes, and surrounding green space is a covariate in the mockup. However, human cognition and neighborhood greenness do not establish benefits of extensive green roofs for bee abundance or species richness.
