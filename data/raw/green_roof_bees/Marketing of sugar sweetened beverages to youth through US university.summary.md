## Title
Marketing of sugar-sweetened beverages to youth through U.S. university pouring rights contracts

## Topics
Youth marketing; sugary drinks; university contracts; sports sponsorship; document content analysis

## Introduction
University beverage contracts may enable marketing to children as well as adult campus populations, raising concerns about promotion of sugar-sweetened beverages.

## Methods
The authors obtained 139 pouring-rights contracts from 132 public universities between June 2019 and August 2020. Two reviewers coded each contract and extracted provisions describing youth-targeted marketing activities.

## Results
Twenty-two contracts (16%) contained 25 youth-related marketing provisions, 24 linked to athletics. Examples included summer camps, children's experiences at university events, and advertising during high-school sports. Five contracts acknowledged potentially applicable restrictions.

## Discussion
The analysis documents contractual permissions, not whether every activity occurred or caused increased beverage consumption. It highlights a policy-relevant marketing pathway but cannot quantify children's exposure or health effects.

## Conclusion
Universities and beverage companies should reconsider youth-targeted provisions, and the authors advocate stronger restrictions on such marketing.
