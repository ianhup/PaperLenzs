## Title
Defining Phenotypes from Clinical Data to Drive Genomic Research

## Topics
Electronic health records; phenotyping; biobanks; natural language processing; GWAS; PheWAS

## Introduction
Linking longitudinal electronic health records to DNA biobanks enables large-scale genomic research, but reliable phenotype definitions are essential to avoid misclassification.

## Methods
This review synthesizes approaches to extracting disease, trait, and drug-response phenotypes from clinical records, including manual chart review, rule-based algorithms, natural language processing, and machine learning. It surveys genomic applications and implementation challenges.

## Results
The reviewed studies show that EHR-derived phenotypes can support genome-wide and phenome-wide association research and expand the range of observable traits. Combining clinical data sources and validating extraction algorithms are central to reliable phenotype construction.

## Discussion
Clinical records reflect healthcare delivery and billing as well as underlying biology. Missingness, variable coding, portability, and data standardization limit generalization; the review does not provide a single new intervention effect.

## Conclusion
Validated, standardized phenotyping and coordinated biobank infrastructure can strengthen genomic discovery and precision-medicine research.

## Relevance to research
Not directly relevant to green-roof ecology. Clinical phenotype extraction does not address bee communities or rooftop covariates. Its data-validation principles may inform a paper-reading system, but it is not ecological support for the mockup.
