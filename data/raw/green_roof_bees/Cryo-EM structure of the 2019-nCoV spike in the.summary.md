## Title
Cryo-EM structure of the 2019-nCoV spike in the prefusion conformation

## Topics
Coronavirus; spike protein; cryo-electron microscopy; ACE2; structural biology; antibody binding

## Introduction
The emerging coronavirus spike protein was an urgent target for vaccines and therapeutics. The study characterizes its prefusion structure and receptor-binding properties.

## Methods
The authors expressed and purified a prefusion-stabilized spike ectodomain, determined its structure using cryo-electron microscopy, and used biophysical binding assays to examine ACE2 affinity and recognition by previously described SARS-CoV antibodies.

## Results
A 3.5-angstrom structure revealed a predominant trimer state with one receptor-binding domain exposed. The spike bound ACE2 more strongly than the SARS-CoV comparator, while tested SARS-CoV receptor-binding-domain antibodies showed little appreciable binding.

## Discussion
The structure identifies accessible conformations and informs countermeasure design. Binding results suggest limited cross-reactivity for the tested antibodies, rather than proving that every SARS-CoV antibody lacks activity.

## Conclusion
The prefusion structure supplies a molecular foundation for developing and evaluating coronavirus vaccines, antibodies, and diagnostics.
