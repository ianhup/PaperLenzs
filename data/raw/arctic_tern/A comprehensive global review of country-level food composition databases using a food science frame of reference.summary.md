## Title
A comprehensive global review of country-level food composition databases using a food science frame of reference

## Topics
Food databases; nutrition; systematic review; data quality; food science

## Introduction
National databases need reliable nutrient profiles that reflect foods actually consumed and their preparation.

## Methods
A systematic global search identified 107 online food-composition databases, assessed across 80 categories of food coverage, processing, nutrient data, and data provenance.

## Results
Most databases cover traditional foods and common preparations; only 20 include meat analogues. Sixty-eight report chemically analyzed values, while 46 include imputed or calculated values.

## Discussion
Coverage and provenance gaps undermine comparisons, especially for newer foods and processing methods.

## Conclusion
The authors call for scientifically sound, locally relevant, clearly documented food databases.

## Relevance to research
Not relevant: these are human nutrition data, not marine prey abundance, Arctic tern feeding records, migration routes, or warming effects.
