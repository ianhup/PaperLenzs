## Title
Effects of air-conditioning operation and high ambient temperature on CO, NOx, and particle number emissions from heavy-duty diesel vehicles under CHTC cycles

## Topics
Vehicle emissions; high temperature; air conditioning; nitrogen oxides; particles

## Introduction
Air conditioning increases engine load in hot weather, potentially altering diesel exhaust emissions.

## Methods
Six heavy-duty vehicles are tested on climate-controlled chassis dynamometers at 32°C and 42°C with air conditioning on or off, using Chinese heavy-duty driving cycles and emissions measurements.

## Results
At 32°C, air conditioning increased distance-based particle-number emissions for all six vehicles, while carbon-monoxide changes were smaller and nitrogen-oxide responses varied by vehicle.

## Discussion
Emission effects depend on vehicle type, pollutant, stage of driving, and ambient temperature; controlled laboratory tests may not cover every real-world situation.

## Conclusion
Heat and auxiliary-load effects should be considered in heavy-duty vehicle emissions assessment.

## Relevance to research
Very indirect climate relevance: transport emissions relate to broader anthropogenic atmospheric change, but the paper neither quantifies global warming trends nor analyzes Arctic tern GPS routes, winds, ocean temperature, or fish prey.
