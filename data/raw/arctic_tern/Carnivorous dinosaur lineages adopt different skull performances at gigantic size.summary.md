## Title
Carnivorous dinosaur lineages adopt different skull performances at gigantic size

## Topics
Theropod dinosaurs; finite-element analysis; biomechanics; feeding ecology

## Introduction
Giant carnivorous dinosaur groups evolved different mechanical solutions for capturing prey.

## Methods
The study uses three-dimensional finite-element analysis of theropod skulls, comparing stress and bite-force-related performance across lineages and standardized sizes.

## Results
Large tyrannosaurids show increasing bite-force-related skull stress with size, whereas megalosauroids and allosauroids tend to maintain lower-stress configurations.

## Discussion
The biomechanical contrasts suggest different ecological feeding strategies rather than a single path to gigantism.

## Conclusion
Tyrannosaurids traded higher cranial stress for strong bites, unlike other giant theropods.

## Relevance to research
Not relevant: prehistoric dinosaur skull mechanics cannot answer how warming changes Arctic tern routes, weather exposure, prey distribution, or current survival.
