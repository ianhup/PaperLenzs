## Title
Cold atmospheric plasma as a testable redox input in plants: Chemistry, biological dose and evidence requirements

## Topics
Cold atmospheric plasma; plants; redox signaling; exposure measurement; review

## Introduction
Plasma-generated reactive compounds can change plant physiology, but equal treatment times need not imply equal chemical dose.

## Methods
The authors critically synthesize plant studies, define a multidimensional biological-redox-dose descriptor, assess mechanistic evidence, and use a published wheat dataset as an illustration.

## Results
The review identifies measurement gaps and alternative explanations involving nitrate nutrition, acidity, and surface treatment; it does not establish a universal dose-response relationship.

## Discussion
Chemically matched controls and plant-state measurements are needed before attributing outcomes specifically to redox signaling.

## Conclusion
Plasma is promising as an experimental agricultural input but needs rigorous validation.

## Relevance to research
Not relevant: the article concerns plant chemistry and agricultural treatments, not avian migration, marine prey fish, or ocean warming.
