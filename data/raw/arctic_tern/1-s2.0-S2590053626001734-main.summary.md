## Title
Beyond keywords: Semantic classification with large language models for mapping synthetic biology literature

## Topics
Fine-tuned language models; semantic classification; bibliometrics; literature retrieval

## Introduction
Keyword-only searches can miss interdisciplinary synthetic-biology publications whose subject matter is described in varied language.

## Methods
Researchers pair greedy/frequency-based high-recall keyword selection with a fine-tuned language-model classifier, then validate on curated papers, an entire specialist journal, and a large negative corpus.

## Results
The classifier achieved macro-F1 of 0.97; the pipeline recovered 57.81% of journal articles versus 27.20% for single-keyword retrieval, with a 0.76% false-positive rate on borderline negatives.

## Discussion
Semantic classification improves topic coverage, although estimates come from synthetic-biology corpora and specific validation choices.

## Conclusion
The approach may generalize to mapping other interdisciplinary literatures.

## Relevance to research
Indirectly relevant to the research workflow: adapting this method could help identify papers about warming, seabird migration, and prey fish even without exact keywords. The reported classifier does not analyze tern behavior itself.
