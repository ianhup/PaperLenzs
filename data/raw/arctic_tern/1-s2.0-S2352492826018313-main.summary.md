## Title
Chemistry-related interpretive and reporting errors in geopolymer research: A critical diagnostic review

## Topics
Geopolymers; chemical interpretation; reporting standards; reproducibility

## Introduction
Chemical comparisons in geopolymer research can be misleading if different precursors, activators, or ratios are treated as equivalent.

## Methods
This critical diagnostic review examines six categories of interpretive risk, compares selected review literature, and illustrates twelve claim-specific assessments and a hypothetical formulation example.

## Results
The main product is a reporting and evidence-evaluation framework distinguishing observational support, causal strength, and generalizability, not a pooled estimate of error frequency.

## Discussion
The review warns against overclaiming material mechanisms from incomplete chemical reporting.

## Conclusion
Explicit chemical-system definitions and evidence-proportional conclusions can improve reproducibility.

## Relevance to research
Not directly relevant: geopolymer chemistry has no substantive link to tern migration. General advice on careful causal interpretation is broadly applicable but supplies no climate or bird evidence.
