## Title
Dairy farmers’ attitudes and empathy and cattle’s reactions to humans in cow-calf contact systems compared to early separation

## Topics
Dairy welfare; cow-calf contact; empathy; animal avoidance behavior

## Introduction
Rearing calves with their mothers may change farmers’ attitudes toward cattle and cattle responses to humans.

## Methods
The study compares 25 cow–calf contact farms with 25 early-separation farms, surveying farmers (57 per group), measuring cattle avoidance distance, and using regression and mixed models.

## Results
Cow–calf contact farmers expressed more positive attitudes; avoidance distances were broadly similar for calves and cows, while heifers in contact systems avoided humans at greater distances.

## Discussion
Relationships among empathy, handling attitudes, and cattle behavior are complex, not uniformly explained by the rearing system.

## Conclusion
Cow–calf contact is associated with positive farmer attitudes but does not consistently improve animal approach behavior.

## Relevance to research
Not relevant: dairy management and domestic animal responses to people do not address climate-driven tern migrations or prey fish changes.
