## Title
Neuro-behavioral influence of ethnocentrism on beer consumers from different countries residing in Costa Rica

## Topics
Consumer ethnocentrism; neuromarketing; eye tracking; purchasing behavior

## Introduction
Brand-country associations can affect consumer decisions, raising questions about cultural differences and unconscious responses.

## Methods
An exploratory nonprobability sample of 146 residents representing four nationalities is tested with eye tracking, facial coding, implicit associations, CETSCALE, and partial-least-squares structural-equation modeling.

## Results
Ethnocentric tendencies varied across nationality groups; Costa Rican and U.S. participants were lower, while Mexican and Nicaraguan participants were higher within this sample.

## Discussion
Convergence across biometric and traditional measures supports the study-specific consumer model, with sampling limitations.

## Conclusion
The findings may inform international branding strategies.

Not relevant: the subject is human beer purchasing, not bird migration, global warming, ocean temperatures, or fish availability.
