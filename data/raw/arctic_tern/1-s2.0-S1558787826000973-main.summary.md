## Title
Caregiver bereavement-related distress and surviving dog behavior after pet dog loss: Associations with euthanasia and expected death in a cross-sectional study from Italy and the UK

## Topics
Pet bereavement; dog behavior; human–animal relationships; cross-sectional survey

## Introduction
Loss of a companion dog may influence the caregiver’s grief and reported behavior of another dog living in the household.

## Methods
An online Mourning Dog Questionnaire incorporating five psychometric scales was completed by 655 dog caregivers in Italy and the UK; multiple regressions assessed death circumstances and behavior.

## Results
Expected death and euthanasia predicted less caregiver grief distress; higher caregiver grief and anger correlated with reports of appetite, vocalization, fear, or attention changes in surviving dogs.

## Discussion
Questionnaire and cross-sectional designs limit causal interpretations; cultural and caregiver influences may shape reports.

## Conclusion
Veterinary end-of-life care could support both grieving caregivers and surviving pets.

## Relevance to research
Not relevant: domestic dog bereavement does not address migratory birds, temperature/wind changes, ocean prey, or survival during long-distance flight.
