## Title
Different facets of diversity reveal different patterns of fish assemblages in tropical rivers: The Buyuan River (China)

## Topics
Fish biodiversity; taxonomic diversity; functional diversity; environmental drivers

## Introduction
Understanding fish assemblages requires more than species counts because functional and phylogenetic diversity may respond differently to habitat conditions.

## Methods
Researchers assess alpha and beta taxonomic, phylogenetic, and functional diversity in the Buyuan River and relate them to environmental and spatial variables using correlations, redundancy analysis, and variation partitioning.

## Results
Diversity dimensions showed partially independent patterns. Environmental variables explained notable portions of taxonomic turnover and beta diversity, while spatial contributions differed by component.

## Discussion
The results suggest that habitat conditions and dispersal structure influence fish communities in different ways.

## Conclusion
Conservation planning should include multiple diversity measures rather than relying on species richness alone.

## Relevance to research
Indirect ecological relevance: changes in fish communities can affect food webs, resembling the mock project’s hypothesis that warmer oceans alter Arctic tern prey. This study examines tropical freshwater fish, not small marine fish along Arctic tern migration routes or warming effects on terns.
