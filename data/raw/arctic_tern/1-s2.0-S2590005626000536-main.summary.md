## Title
Multimodal artificial intelligence for human–computer interaction enhanced breast lesion diagnosis

## Topics
Multimodal AI; ultrasound; Transformers; human–AI collaboration; diagnosis

## Introduction
Breast ultrasound interpretation may improve when images, lesion-specific features, clinical records, and clinician decisions are considered jointly.

## Methods
The authors develop KIDNet for lesion detection/scoring, fuse imaging and clinical variables with a Transformer, and compare alternative human-computer image-selection modes.

## Results
Multimodal fusion reached 93.1% diagnostic accuracy; collaborative image selection reached 95.4% in the reported evaluation.

## Discussion
The results illustrate benefits from combining complementary evidence with expert input, within a medical-imaging context.

## Conclusion
Integrating human decision-making and multiple data streams may improve ultrasound-assisted diagnosis.

## Relevance to research
Indirect methodological relevance: a multimodal framework could combine tern tracking, temperatures, winds, and prey observations. The measured diagnostic accuracy is medical-specific and not evidence of migration change.
