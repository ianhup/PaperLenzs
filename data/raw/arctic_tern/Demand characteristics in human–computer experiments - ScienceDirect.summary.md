## Title
Demand characteristics in human–computer experiments

## Topics
Experimental bias; human-computer interaction; participant expectations; validity

## Introduction
People in experiments may infer the researcher’s hypothesis and change responses, producing apparent effects that are not caused by the tested interface.

## Methods
Two studies examine expectations in a keyboard-performance evaluation and a virtual-reality body-ownership scenario.

## Results
Belief that a keyboard was research-based boosted reported experience and performance; hypothesis cues also elicited expected virtual-reality responses without the purported illusion.

## Discussion
Demand characteristics may bias both objective and subjective measures even in automated experiments.

## Conclusion
Human-computer studies should anticipate, measure, and reduce expectation-driven responses.

## Relevance to research
Not directly relevant: wild Arctic tern tracking does not involve subjects responding to human experimental expectations. General anti-bias principles may assist research design but provide no climate or ecology evidence.
