## Title
A continental record of the late Campanian negative carbon isotopic excursion (LCE, late Campanian event) in the Dinosaur Park Formation, Alberta, Canada

## Topics
Paleoclimate; carbon isotopes; Cretaceous; sedimentology; carbon cycling

## Introduction
A known Late Cretaceous carbon-cycle disturbance has mostly been documented in marine strata; the paper tests whether it is also visible in land deposits.

## Methods
The team measures stable carbon isotopes in fossil organic matter from radiometrically dated Dinosaur Park Formation floodplain sediments and compares the signal to marine chronologies.

## Results
A negative excursion of roughly 0.8 per mille appears in terrestrial material and is interpreted as a record of the global Late Campanian event.

## Discussion
Tests of organic-matter composition and alteration support an atmospheric carbon-cycle interpretation rather than a local preservation artifact.

## Conclusion
Carbon-cycle disturbances can be correlated between continental and marine geological archives.

## Relevance to research
Very low contextual relevance: it illustrates how climate/carbon-system signals can be reconstructed, but concerns an ancient geological interval, not modern warming, Arctic tern movements, or present-day prey fish.
