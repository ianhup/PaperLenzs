## Title
Regional seawater-chemistry controls on ocean alkalinity enhancement efficiency: Carbonate precipitation risk under pH-equilibrated OAE

## Topics
Ocean carbon removal; seawater chemistry; carbonates; regional modeling

## Introduction
Ocean alkalinity enhancement is a proposed carbon-removal strategy, but carbonate precipitation can undermine carbon storage.

## Methods
Researchers use regional near-surface seawater conditions from GLODAPv2.2023 and calculate aragonite saturation under baseline and added dissolved-inorganic-carbon scenarios of 500, 1,000, and 2,000 micromoles per kilogram.

## Results
Warm, saline, relatively high-pH waters more readily approach potentially excessive aragonite saturation; several regions reach higher-risk conditions at the 2,000-micromole scenario.

## Discussion
A uniform treatment dose can have different chemical consequences across oceans; carbonate precipitation risk is site-specific.

## Conclusion
Ocean carbon-removal design should evaluate local chemistry and saturation risk.

## Relevance to research
Indirectly relevant to the climate–ocean context: it studies seawater properties and carbon mitigation, potentially important for marine ecosystems. It does not test temperature-driven prey-fish redistribution or changes in Arctic tern migrations.
