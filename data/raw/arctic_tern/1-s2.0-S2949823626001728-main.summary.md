## Title
Nature-based solutions for natural hazards in Costa Rica: Evidence, gaps, and challenges

## Topics
Climate adaptation; nature-based solutions; disaster risk; conservation; Costa Rica

## Introduction
Nature-based approaches can reduce floods, erosion, and landslides while yielding ecological benefits, but Costa Rican evidence is fragmented.

## Methods
Researchers conduct systematic searches across scholarly databases and local repositories, map publication patterns, and code interventions and reported outcomes using nature-based-solution frameworks.

## Results
Publications and reported projects increased after 2007. Most interventions involve forests and watersheds; consistent long-term effectiveness and equity measures remain scarce.

## Discussion
Limited financing, fragmented governance, and inconsistent maintenance constrain implementation.

## Conclusion
The review recommends stronger evaluation and interagency planning for nature-based hazard mitigation.

## Relevance to research
Low contextual relevance: it concerns climate-related ecosystem management and research gaps but mainly terrestrial hazards in Costa Rica, not the Arctic tern’s oceanic migration, seasonal timing, or fish prey.
