## Title
Early positive drain cultures predict major morbidity independent of postoperative pancreatic fistula formation after pancreatoduodenectomy

## Topics
Pancreatic surgery; postoperative infection; risk stratification; drain cultures

## Introduction
Early surgical drain cultures may predict complications beyond traditional pancreatic-fistula risk markers.

## Methods
A retrospective review of 449 pancreatoduodenectomy patients uses cultures collected by postoperative day three, multivariable logistic regression, and an amylase-stratified subgroup analysis.

## Results
Positive cultures occurred in 13.8% of patients and coincided with 33.9% major morbidity versus 14.7% with negative cultures; adjusted odds ratio was about 3.04.

## Discussion
The association suggests independent predictive value but does not prove that infection causes the complications.

## Conclusion
Early microbiological monitoring may improve postoperative risk assessment.
