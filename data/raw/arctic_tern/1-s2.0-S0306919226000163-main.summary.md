## Title
Modelled health impacts of three stakeholder-selected policies to support healthy and environmentally sustainable population diets in New Zealand

## Topics
Sustainable diets; food policy; public health; health equity; simulation

## Introduction
The paper asks whether policy changes encouraging healthier, more sustainable diets could improve health in New Zealand.

## Methods
A proportional multistate life-table model projects lifetime health-adjusted life years, health-system costs, and ethnic equity under three stakeholder-selected interventions.

## Results
A GST exemption for core sustainable foods yields about 87,410 health-adjusted life years and NZ$927.3 million in modeled health-system savings; education gains are smaller, while gardens generate about 8,040 life years.

## Discussion
The modeled gains depend on assumptions and implementation choices; the study anticipates relatively greater per-person benefit for Māori.

## Conclusion
Tax and education interventions may be cost-saving, while community-garden policy requires additional investment.

## Relevance to research
Not directly relevant: sustainability is shared vocabulary, but the outcomes are human diet, costs, and disease rather than Arctic tern migration or marine prey responses to climate change.
