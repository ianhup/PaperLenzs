## Title
Integrated chromosome-level assembly and resequencing uncover the genetic basis of fruit size differentiation in Russian olive

## Topics
Russian olive; whole-genome sequencing; fruit size; selective sweeps; plant genetics

## Introduction
Russian olive has large- and small-fruited forms, but the genetic basis of their fruit-size differences is incompletely understood.

## Methods
The researchers combine nanopore and Illumina sequencing with Hi-C genome assembly, resequence 70 Xinjiang accessions, test selective sweeps, and validate selected genes using transgenic Arabidopsis.

## Results
They assemble a 505-Mb genome containing 33,650 predicted protein-coding genes, identify 1,444 selection-associated genes and 19 fruit-size candidates, and functionally test four candidates.

## Discussion
The identified loci suggest molecular pathways controlling traits useful for cultivar improvement, although candidate associations require cautious interpretation.

## Conclusion
The genomic resource can guide molecular breeding of Russian olive.

## Relevance to research
Not relevant to the tern project: this paper concerns fruit-size genetics in a tree. It does not investigate climate-driven avian movements or prey availability.
