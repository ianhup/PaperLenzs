## Title
Genetic diversity, population structure, and dispersal patterns of wild and cultivated Cycas szechuanensis in China

## Topics
Conservation genetics; endangered plants; seed dispersal; pollen flow

## Introduction
The endemic cycad Cycas szechuanensis requires conservation informed by genetic diversity and dispersal between natural and cultivated populations.

## Methods
The authors genotype 622 individuals across known locations using 19 microsatellite markers, analyze population structure and spatial genetic patterns, and estimate parentage and dispersal in major populations.

## Results
Wild populations show relatively high genetic diversity but some inbreeding; four geographic genetic groups emerge. Seed movement is limited while inferred pollen dispersal reaches about 1.9 km.

## Discussion
Different pollen and seed dispersal scales shape recruitment patterns and the genetic isolation of plant groups.

## Conclusion
Protection of wild populations and gene flow is important for cycad conservation.

## Relevance to research
Low methodological relevance: tracking dispersal and population structure offers a conservation analogy, but plant pollen/seed processes differ from migratory flight and do not reveal warming-induced Arctic tern route changes.
