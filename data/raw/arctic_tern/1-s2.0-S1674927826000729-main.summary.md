## Title
Using appropriate methods to assess population exposure to extreme weather events: Why and how?

## Topics
Extreme weather; exposure modeling; dynamic movement; climate adaptation; review

## Introduction
Quantifying exposure to extreme weather is difficult when event thresholds and affected-population measurements vary across studies.

## Methods
This methodological review compares absolute, relative, and composite thresholds and static, dynamic, and alternative-indicator approaches to measuring human exposure.

## Results
It identifies multi-factor composite thresholds and movement-aware dynamic exposure measures as promising, while noting inconsistent definitions and incomplete treatment of vulnerability.

## Discussion
Integrating real-time movement, hazards, and vulnerability can outperform purely stationary exposure estimates; the review concerns humans, not birds.

## Conclusion
The authors advocate harmonized, multi-factor exposure assessment frameworks.

## Relevance to research
Moderate methodological relevance: a dynamic exposure framework could combine tracked Arctic tern positions with temperatures, winds, and extreme-weather events. Its human-population results do not establish tern migration impacts or the mock study’s reported route changes.
