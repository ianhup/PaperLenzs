## Title
Dynamic Adjustment Design of Intelligent Landscape Environmental Comfort from the Perspective of Human-Computer Interaction

## Topics
Environmental sensors; comfort prediction; LSTM; random forest; reinforcement learning

## Introduction
Outdoor comfort systems often lack responsive adaptation to changing environmental conditions and user needs.

## Methods
The proposed closed-loop system combines measurements of temperature, humidity, light, noise, and physiological responses with hybrid LSTM/random-forest prediction and reinforcement-learning control.

## Results
Across seven reported scenarios, average comfort was 7.6 points, demand satisfaction 91.8%, and daily energy use 9.6 kWh.

## Discussion
Multi-source sensing and adaptive decisions are proposed to personalize landscape equipment behavior; outcomes are from comfort settings.

## Conclusion
The authors present an adaptive environment-control architecture.

## Relevance to research
Weak methodological relevance: its sensor-fusion approach could inspire combining bird GPS, weather, and prey data, but comfort scores and control algorithms do not test Arctic tern migratory responses.
