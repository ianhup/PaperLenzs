## Title
Do coupled population, economic, and land use dynamics explain household energy transitions in Malawi?

## Topics
Malawi; charcoal; land-use change; forest loss; household energy

## Introduction
Rural energy transitions matter for livelihoods and deforestation, particularly where charcoal is replacing firewood.

## Methods
Four waves of national household surveys are linked with satellite forest-cover records to examine fuel choice and forest change between 2004 and 2020.

## Results
Rural charcoal use rose from 3.9% to 13.4%; smaller, younger-headed, relatively asset-rich but land-poor households had greater odds of charcoal use. Some protected areas lost more than 20% of forest cover.

## Discussion
The association between livelihood conditions, energy markets, and forest loss supports integrated policy rather than treating fuel choice in isolation.

## Conclusion
Energy planning and natural-resource management should be coordinated.

## Relevance to research
Low indirect relevance: environmental degradation and land-use change can interact with climate, but this study is about Malawian household fuels and forests, not Arctic tern migration, marine fish distribution, or ocean warming.
