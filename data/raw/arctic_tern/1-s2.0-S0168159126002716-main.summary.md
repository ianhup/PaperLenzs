## Title
How does the behaviour of bio-detection dogs correlate with handler decision-making?

## Topics
Detection dogs; behavioral signals; observer agreement; handler decisions

## Introduction
A handler must judge whether a detection dog has identified a target, but the behavioral cues used for those decisions can be ambiguous.

## Methods
Eight blinded raters evaluated 84 videos of 11 disease-detection dogs, recording indication classifications, certainty, and response strength; dog behaviors were also objectively coded.

## Results
Raters achieved only moderate agreement. Sitting and sustained contact with a sample were clearer target-related cues than subtler movements such as head knocks.

## Discussion
Variation among human interpretations can introduce inconsistency into canine training data and decision records.

## Conclusion
More explicit interpretation criteria and attention to subtle cues could improve reliability.

## Relevance to research
Not topically relevant: the experiments concern detection-dog training, not tern navigation or climate. Observer-consistency principles could be borrowed for any manual coding of bird behavior, but the paper provides no Arctic tern evidence.
