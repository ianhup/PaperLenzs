## Title
Could artificial intelligence improve astronaut debriefing processes? A review from psychological and human–computer interaction perspectives

## Topics
Artificial intelligence; astronauts; debriefing; psychology; human-computer interaction

## Introduction
Astronaut debriefings depend on memory and human facilitation, potentially losing information from demanding missions.

## Methods
This perspective article draws on psychological theory, human–computer interaction, and emerging large-language-model capabilities; it is not a controlled trial.

## Results
The authors identify opportunities for transcription, multimodal data integration, structured feedback, and self-reflection while flagging privacy and oversight challenges.

## Discussion
The practical effectiveness of AI-enabled debriefing remains a proposal requiring empirical testing.

## Conclusion
Human supervision and ethical safeguards are necessary if AI is incorporated into astronaut training.

## Relevance to research
Not relevant to climate effects on Arctic terns. AI-assisted synthesis could support a research workflow, but this paper contains no bird-tracking, environmental, or marine ecological results.
