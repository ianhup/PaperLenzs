## Title
A research framework to investigate food systems at a national scale

## Topics
Food systems; ecological economics; political economy; sustainability frameworks

## Introduction
National food systems connect economic institutions, agricultural production, ecological pressures, and social reproduction.

## Methods
The authors critically review literature and integrate food-regime theory, social-metabolism approaches, and feminist economic concepts into a six-dimension, 34-element analytical framework.

## Results
The output is a conceptual research framework spanning governance, supply chains, socioecological impacts, social reproduction, and mechanisms of change rather than new empirical estimates.

## Discussion
The framework argues that cheap food production and existing economic regimes complicate a sustainable transition.

## Conclusion
Understanding food-system transformation requires economic, ecological, and social analyses together.

## Relevance to research
Low relevance: systems thinking may inform how to frame interactions among climate, prey fisheries, and biodiversity, but this conceptual article supplies no Arctic tern tracking or marine-ecology data.
